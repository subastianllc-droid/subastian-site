Black Friday SaaS Deals: How to Avoid Annual-Plan Renewal Traps

By Subastian · Updated November 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Black Friday is the one week a year when software companies push annual plans the hardest. The discount is usually real. The catch is that it often covers only the first year, and the plan renews on its own twelve months later at whatever the price is then. You do not need to skip the deals. You need four checks before you pay, and one reminder after.

The short version
  • Black Friday is Friday, November 27, 2026, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Most SaaS deals that week are on annual plans.
  • Before you buy, check four things: weekly use, duplicate tools, the renewal price and date, and the cancel and refund terms.
  • If a deal fails two of the four checks, skip it.
  • Put the renewal date on your calendar before you close the checkout tab.

Why annual Black Friday deals turn into renewal traps

Three things stack up against you:

  1. The discount is on the first term. Read the wording next to the price: “first year,” “first billing cycle” or “for 12 months.” After that, the plan usually renews at the standard rate.
  2. The plan renews automatically. A monthly charge gets noticed twelve times a year. An annual charge comes around once, and by then nobody remembers why it was bought. Our 30-7-3-1 reminder schedule explains why annual renewals are the expensive ones.
  3. You buy under a deadline. Countdown timers and “ends Monday” banners make a tool you would use someday feel urgent today. A year later, it renews for a tool nobody opened.

None of this makes the deals bad. It means the renewal, not the Black Friday price, is the real price.

The 4 checks before you buy an annual deal

Run these on every annual offer, even from a tool you like:

  1. Did we use this tool at least weekly over the last month? Weekly use is the best sign a tool will still earn its keep in month eleven. If you have not started using it yet, try it on a free trial or one monthly payment first. A year up front is the most expensive way to test a tool. Our guide to stopping free trials from charging you keeps that test from turning into a charge.
  2. Do we already pay for something that does the same job? Common overlaps are two schedulers, two email tools, two AI writing assistants, or a design app plus the editor built into another tool. Agencies should also look for seats left over from finished client work. See how to find duplicate subscriptions and how to track software costs per client.
  3. What does it renew at after year one, and when? Look in the checkout summary, the pricing page footnotes, the order confirmation email and the terms. Screenshot what you find. If you cannot find a renewal price, ask support in writing before you pay. Then put the renewal date on your calendar now.
  4. Can we cancel or get a refund in the first 30 days? Find the refund policy and screenshot it on the day you buy, because pages change. Note whether cancelling stops only the renewal or also ends your access right away. If the promo says “final sale” or “non-refundable,” count this check as failed.

The rule: if a deal fails two of the four, skip it. If it fails only check 3, wait until you know the renewal price.

Red flags in Black Friday deal terms

  • A first-year price with no renewal price shown. If the page only says “introductory” or “first year,” the renewal price is somewhere else, or nowhere.
  • A monthly number billed yearly. “$12/mo, billed annually” is one charge for the full year, up front.
  • A bigger tier or more seats than you need. A discount on a plan two sizes too big is still a higher bill at renewal.
  • “Final sale” or “non-refundable” on the promo. That removes your safety net if the tool does not fit.
  • No self-serve cancel. If you can only cancel by emailing or calling support, start that process well before the renewal date.

What to do the day you buy

  1. Screenshot the checkout summary, the renewal price and the refund terms.
  2. Save the receipt in one folder or email label you use for every subscription.
  3. Add two calendar reminders: 30 days and 7 days before the renewal. The 30-7-3-1 schedule adds 3-day and 1-day reminders if you want them.
  4. Add the tool to your subscription list, with its renewal date, price and who uses it.
  5. Set a 60-day check-in. If nobody is using it weekly by then, turn off auto-renew while you still have time to decide.

Already bought an annual plan you don’t use?

  • Check the refund window today. It usually counts from the purchase date, not from when you started using the tool.
  • Turn off auto-renew. In most tools you keep access until the paid term ends. Confirm that in the billing settings before you click.
  • Ask before you cancel. Some vendors offer a downgrade or a pause when you ask. Our renewal reminders guide covers what to ask.
  • Charged after cancelling? Follow the steps in how to stop free trials from charging you, which covers charges that land after you have cancelled.

Do auto-renewal laws protect you?

Some, if you bought for yourself, and less if you bought for a business.

California’s Automatic Renewal Law is one of the strictest. For plans with a first term of a year or longer, the seller must send a notice 15 to 45 days before renewal. Price changes need notice 7 to 30 days ahead, and a plan bought online must be cancellable online (Cooley). But the law defines a consumer as someone buying “for personal, family, or household purposes” (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17601). A tool your agency buys on a business card may not be covered.

At the federal level, the FTC’s 2024 click-to-cancel rule was struck down by a federal appeals court in 2025. The FTC restarted rulemaking in March 2026, and no replacement rule is in effect yet (FTC, Jones Day).

The protection you control is your own calendar. This section is general information, not legal advice.

Black Friday SaaS deals: FAQ

When is Black Friday 2026?

Friday, November 27, 2026. Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30.

Are Black Friday SaaS deals worth it?

They can be, for a tool you already use weekly that doesn’t duplicate another tool, with a renewal price you know and a refund window you’ve read. If a deal fails two of those four checks, skip it.

What happens when a Black Friday annual plan ends?

Unless the terms say otherwise, it renews automatically for another year. The Black Friday discount often doesn’t carry over, so check the renewal price in the terms before you buy.

Can I get a refund on an annual SaaS plan?

Only if the vendor’s terms allow it. Find the refund window before you pay, screenshot it, and note the date it closes.

Should I turn off auto-renew right after buying?

If you’re unsure about the tool, it’s a safe default. In most tools you keep access until the term ends and can turn renewal back on. Check first that cancelling doesn’t end access right away.

Is a monthly or annual plan better?

Monthly costs more per month but lets you stop any month. Annual makes sense once you’ve used a tool monthly long enough to know you’ll keep it.

How far ahead should I set a renewal reminder?

At least 30 days and 7 days before the renewal date. That leaves time to compare, negotiate or cancel.
