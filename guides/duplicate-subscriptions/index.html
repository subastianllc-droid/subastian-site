How to Find Duplicate Subscriptions (Two Tools Doing the Same Job)

By Subastian · Updated October 6, 2026 · 3 min read

The easiest subscription waste to miss is not the tool nobody uses. It is two tools that each get used a little because they do the same job. Neither looks wasteful on its own. Together, they are paying twice. Here is how to find them.

The short version
  • Overlap is about the job, not the brand: two tools for the same task.
  • Check each category on the list below for overlapping tools.
  • Keep the one you actually open and export your data before cancelling the other.
  • Before buying anything new, check what you already own.

What counts as a duplicate

  • Two tools for the same job. Two project managers, two email platforms, two cloud-storage plans.
  • A tool you pay for separately that is already included in another plan you have.
  • Overlapping add-ons. A paid add-on inside one tool that does what another subscription already does.

A category checklist

List your subscriptions first (our 15-minute audit shows how), then go category by category and ask whether you have more than one tool doing the job:

CategoryOverlap to look for
AI toolsSeveral AI chat or writing subscriptions, plus AI add-ons inside tools you already pay for
AnalyticsMore than one tool tracking the same website or app
AutomationMore than one platform connecting the same apps
Cloud storageSeveral storage plans, personal and work, holding the same files
CommunicationSeparate paid chat and video-meeting tools when one of them covers both
CRMA CRM plus another contact database or spreadsheet service

Email marketingTwo platforms sending newsletters or campaigns
Funnels & landing pagesA page builder and a funnel builder that both publish landing pages
Hosting & domainsDomains or hosting split across providers for the same site
Project managementA task tool and a project board used by the same team

How to decide which one to keep

  1. Check which one you actually open. Usage tells you more than the feature list.
  2. List the features you really use, not the ones that exist. The cheaper tool often covers them.
  3. Compare the real price, including the per-seat cost for your whole team and the annual versus monthly rate.
  4. Consider where your data lives and how hard it would be to move. Moving a lot of data is a real cost.
  5. Export your data first, then stop using the tool you are dropping for a couple of weeks before you cancel it. If nothing breaks, cancel.

Stop duplicates before they start

The cheapest duplicate to fix is the one you never buy. Before adding a tool, ask one question: do I already own something that does this? Keep your list somewhere you will actually look, and make that check part of every purchase, including free trials.
