Never Miss a Subscription Renewal: The 30-7-3-1 Reminder Schedule

By Subastian · Updated October 6, 2026 · 3 min read

A monthly charge gets noticed because it comes around again quickly. Annual renewals are different. They are bigger, they appear once a year, and by then you have probably forgotten you signed up. A few well-timed reminders turn a surprise charge into a decision.

The short version
  • Annual plans are the most expensive ones to forget.
  • Set reminders 30, 7, 3 and 1 days out, plus the day of renewal.
  • Use each reminder for a specific action, not just a nudge.
  • Find renewal dates in receipts, billing pages and old statements.

Why annual renewals are the problem

The more rarely something happens, the easier it is to forget. A tool that renews yearly charges a large amount once, long after you stopped thinking about it. The same plan billed monthly would have given you twelve chances to notice it. That is why an annual renewal deserves a reminder system, not just good intentions.

The schedule

Five reminders, each with a job. The point is that every reminder has an action attached, so the series gives you time to do something about the renewal:

WhenWhat to do
30 days beforeDecide: keep, downgrade or cancel. This is your main decision point, with plenty of time.
7 days beforeConfirm the decision. Check for a price change. If you are cancelling, export anything you need.
3 days beforeBuffer. Handle anything that needs a reply from support or someone else's approval.
1 day beforeFinal check. If you are not keeping it, cancel now.
Day of renewalVerify: either the charge matches what you expected or the cancellation went through.

How to set it up

You do not need special software. Two approaches work:

  • Calendar events. Create one yearly repeating event per subscription, then add the reminders at 30, 7, 3 and 1 days. Put the price and the cancellation link in the event notes.
  • A spreadsheet. Keep a column for each renewal date. If the renewal date is in cell B2, the 30-day reminder date is =B2-30, and the others are =B2-7, =B2-3 and =B2-1. Sort the sheet by the next reminder date and work from the top.

Where to find renewal dates

  • Receipts and email. Search your inbox for the service name and “receipt” or “renews”.
  • The account’s billing page, which usually shows the next billing date.
  • Your card statement. An annual charge appears on the same date a year earlier.
  • App-store subscription lists on your phone.

Try asking before you cancel

If you still use a tool but the price bothers you, ask about a lower plan or a discount before you cancel. Some companies offer one to keep a customer. It costs nothing to ask, and a note in your 30-day reminder that says “ask for a better price” makes you do it.
